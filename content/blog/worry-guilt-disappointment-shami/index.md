## Disappointing someone: خيّب أمل

{{< word ar="بخيّب أمل" sh_ipa="ˈbxaj.jeb ʔamal" meaning="I disappoint (someone); from خيّب أمل, to let someone's hope down" root="خ · ي · ب" pos="verb phrase" note="In the diary: «كأني عم بخيّب أمل أهلي» — as if I were disappointing my family. Whoever is disappointed goes right after أمل: أمل أهلي، أملك، أملي." audio="/audio/bikhayyib-amal.mp3" >}}

The same entry also uses **خاب أمل**. **خيّبت أملي** means "You disappointed me." **خاب أملي** means "I was disappointed" — literally, "my hope fell through." Someone can still have caused it; the grammar just presents the hope as failing rather than someone actively disappointing another person. To say who disappointed me, add فيك: **خاب أملي فيك**.

In the diary: «أخاف يخيب أمل الناس اللي بحبوني فيي» — "I'm afraid the people who love me will be disappointed in me." (فيي = "in me.")

## Being hard on yourself: بعذّب حالي

{{< word ar="بعذّب حالي" sh_ipa="baˈʕaz.zeb ħaːli" meaning="I'm tormenting myself; I'm putting myself through misery" root="ع · ذ · ب" pos="verb + reflexive" note="The diary's key line: «بس أغلب هاد كان أنا عم بعذّب حالي.» حال + a suffix works as '-self': حالي، حالك، حاله." audio="/audio/bi3azzib-hali.mp3" >}}

It's stronger than "being hard on myself": عذّب is to torment, so the phrase means "I'm tormenting myself" or "I'm putting myself through misery."
