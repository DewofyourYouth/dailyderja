These are expressions I'm learning to use in Palestinian/Jordanian Arabic, with examples from [my diary](/blog/guilt-maturity-and-loving-well/) and a few practice sentences of my own. The new examples haven't yet been checked by a native speaker.

Here's the sentence from the diary that holds most of them:

## Worry: أشيل هم

{{< word ar="أشيل هَمّ" sh_ipa="ʔaˈʃiːl hamm" meaning="to worry about; to be anxious about (lit. to carry the worry of)" root="ش · ي · ل" pos="phrase" note="In the diary: «اللي كان يخليني أشيل هم الأعياد» — in that context, “dread the holidays” fits, but the expression itself is milder. The thing you're worried about comes straight after هم." >}}

{{< example ar="لا تشيلي هم، كل شي رح يزبط." en="Don't worry, it'll all work out." tag="fem." >}}
{{< example ar="بشيل هم الامتحان من هلّق." en="I'm already worried about the exam." >}}

## Guilt: أحس بالذنب

{{< word ar="أحسّ بالذنب" meaning="to feel guilty" pos="phrase" note="In the diary: «كنت أحس بالذنب» — I used to feel guilty. حسّ بـ = to feel (something); ذنب = guilt, fault." >}}

{{% examples title="Examples" titleAr="أمثلة" %}}
{{< example ar="بحس بالذنب إني ما اتصلت فيها." en="I feel guilty that I didn't call her." >}}
{{< example ar="ليش حاسس بالذنب؟ إنت ما عملت إشي غلط." en="Why do you feel guilty? You didn't do anything wrong." tag="masc." >}}

## Disappointing someone: خيّب أمل

{{< word ar="بخيّب أمل" sh_ipa="ˈbxaj.jeb ʔamal" meaning="I disappoint (someone); from خيّب أمل, to let someone's hope down" root="خ · ي · ب" pos="verb phrase" note="In the diary: «كأني عم بخيّب أمل أهلي» — as if I were disappointing my family. Whoever is disappointed goes right after أمل: أمل أهلي، أملك، أملي." >}}

The same entry also uses **خاب أمل**. **خيّبت أملي** means "You disappointed me." **خاب أملي** means "I was disappointed" — literally, "my hope fell through." Someone can still have caused it; the grammar just presents the hope as failing rather than someone actively disappointing another person. To say who disappointed me, add فيك: **خاب أملي فيك**.

In the diary: «أخاف يخيب أمل الناس اللي بحبوني فيي» — "I'm afraid the people who love me will be disappointed in me." (فيي = "in me.")

{{% examples title="Examples" titleAr="أمثلة" %}}
{{< example ar="ما بدي أخيّب أملك." en="I don't want to disappoint you." >}}
{{< example ar="خيّبت أملي." en="You disappointed me." tag="masc." >}}
{{< example ar="خيّبتي أملي." en="You disappointed me." tag="fem." note="Levantine feminine past-tense ending: ـتي." >}}
{{< example ar="خاب أملي لما ما إجا." en="I was disappointed when he didn't come." >}}
{{% /examples %}}

## Being hard on yourself: بعذّب حالي

{{< word ar="بعذّب حالي" sh_ipa="baˈʕaz.zeb ħaːli" meaning="I'm tormenting myself; I'm putting myself through misery" root="ع · ذ · ب" pos="verb + reflexive" note="The diary's key line: «بس أغلب هاد كان أنا عم بعذّب حالي.» حال + a suffix works as '-self': حالي، حالك، حاله." >}}

It's stronger than "being hard on myself": عذّب is to torment, so the phrase means "I'm tormenting myself" or "I'm putting myself through misery."

{{% examples title="Examples" titleAr="أمثلة" %}}
{{< example ar="بطّل تعذّب حالك." en="Stop tormenting yourself." tag="masc." >}}
{{< example ar="بطّلي تعذّبي حالك." en="Stop tormenting yourself." tag="fem." >}}
{{% /examples %}}
